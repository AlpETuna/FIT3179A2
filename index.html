Homes, households & the cost of belonging

A place to
call home.

A house, a flat, a room to yourself. A home can take many forms. So can the cost of keeping it.

01 / The homes

One country.
Many ways to live.

Two lenses on housing. The 2021 Census maps every region. Prices to July 2026 and assistance records from March 2026 bring it up to date.

Start at the front door. In 2021, 2.8 million households rented their home.

That is 31% of occupied homes. Your odds of renting depend on the kind of building you live in.

Building type shapes who rents. Location shapes what they pay.

02 / The places

Same country.
Different rental markets.

What is a “region”? A Census area of 30,000 to 130,000 people, grouping nearby suburbs and towns. We compare 335 of them.

Australia’s median weekly rent was $375 in 2021. Half of renting households paid less, half paid more.

But where you live decides what you pay: from $80 a week in remote Arnhem Land to $720 in Manly.

Prices follow place. But a rent figure says nothing about who can afford it.

03 / The trade-offs

A rent figure is
only half the picture.

The same rent can buy a very different life. Melbourne City and Casey – South charge almost the same.

One is a city of apartments with lower incomes. The other is a suburb of family houses. Bendigo is cheaper, and so are its incomes.

Follow three communities.

An inner city, an outer suburb and a regional centre, all in Victoria. Every other region stays grey for context.

Local incomes and rents move together. Measuring pressure needs both, for the same household.

04 / The pressure

Help with the rent.
Pressure that remains.

Who is a “recipient”? A single person or couple, with any children, receiving Rent Assistance. Rental stress means paying over 30% of income in rent.

Rents are still rising: up 9.8% from April 2024 to July 2026.

About 1.4 million recipients get Commonwealth Rent Assistance. How much of their income is left after rent?

1 in 5

of recipients pay over half their income in rent, even after assistance.

Assistance lifts about 430,000 recipients out of rental stress. Without it, 75% would be in stress.

With it, 44% still are: 608,000 recipients over the 30% line.

05 / The upshot

608,000 recipients,
still in stress after help.

Nearly one home in three is rented. Where it stands sets the price: from $80 a week in Arnhem Land to $720 in Manly.

Since 2024, rents have risen faster than prices in general.

Rent Assistance cuts rental stress among recipients from 75% to 44%. But that 44% is 608,000 recipients.

And 264,000 of them still hand over more than half their income in rent.

About the story & the data

Sources & dates

  • ABS 2021 Census General Community Profiles: G02, G36, G37 and G42. Census night: 10 August 2021.
  • ABS ASGS 2021 boundaries, simplified for display.
  • ABS monthly Consumer Price Index, July 2026, Table 3.
  • AIHW Commonwealth Rent Assistance, March 2026, CRA.2–3. AIHW analysis of Department of Social Services data, drawing on Services Australia payment records.

Data tables used

Census: G37 tenure by dwelling (Figures 01, 03, 05, 06); G36 dwelling structure (02); G42 household composition (04); G02 medians (05, 07–10).

Consumer Price Index: Table 3, original monthly series (11). Rent assistance: AIHW CRA.2 characteristics (12, 13) and CRA.3 rental stress level (14).

Methods note

Census figures are a 2021 snapshot. Prices and rent-assistance records are newer and describe different groups. They sit side by side, not as cause and effect.

The three featured communities illustrate different settings. They are not a national sample.

Regions are ABS Statistical Areas Level 3 (SA3). Regional charts show regional medians, each region counting once. They omit areas without data, non-geographic areas and Other Territories.

Census counts are slightly adjusted to protect privacy, so parts may not add exactly to totals.

Every map uses an Albers equal-area projection, so areas compare fairly. The city maps in Figure 05 are enlarged; their 10 km bars show the scale.

Dots & rent assistance

In Figure 06 each SA3’s dots are shared among its Statistical Areas Level 1, about 400 people each. Shares follow their rented homes. Each dot sits at a random point inside its small area.

Rent-assistance figures count “income units”: a single person or couple, with any children, with enough data to measure affordability.

“With assistance” uses (rent − assistance) ÷ gross income. “Without” uses rent ÷ gross income for the same recipients. It is a second calculation, not an earlier measurement.

Price changes are the consumer price index rescaled to April 2024.

Methods, accessibility & acknowledgements

All charts use original Vega or Vega-Lite specifications. Each figure provides its data and a readable specification.

The three-community controls, and clicks on featured dots, update the scatterplot and parallel coordinates through Vega’s View API. Data tables provide an alternative to hovering and to reading colour.
