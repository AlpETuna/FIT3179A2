Two lenses on housing. The 2021 Census provides the latest complete regional picture. Monthly prices to July 2026 and rent-assistance records for March 2026 bring the story forward. Their dates and populations are kept separate.

01 / The homes

One country.
Many ways to live.

Start at the front door. In the 2021 Census, 30.6% of occupied private homes were rented. Owning, borrowing and renting sit alongside a wide range of dwelling types.

The shape of a building tells only part of the story. The next question is where that home stands.

02 / The places

Same country.
Different rental markets.

Australia’s median weekly rent was $375 in 2021. A median is the middle reported value: half fall below it and half above. But a single national figure leaves a lot out.

What is a region here? An SA3 is an ABS statistical area that groups nearby communities. The first map compares 335 of these regions with available rent data. The second uses 88 larger SA4 areas to keep its circles readable.

Read area and people separately.

A large inland region covers more of the map, but may contain fewer rented homes than a small city region. That is why colour shows local prices in one map, while circle area shows rented homes in the next.

The histogram adds another perspective: how common each regional median is. Each region counts once, regardless of its population.

National maps use an Australian Albers equal-area projection. Local insets use Mercator for a clear street-scale orientation. Insets have different scales.

04 / The pressure

Help with the rent.
Pressure that remains.

Now move beyond the Census snapshot. Recent price data tracks change, while Commonwealth Rent Assistance records show how costs affect eligible recipients.

Who is counted in the assistance charts? An income unit is a single person or a couple, with any dependent children, who share financial resources. More than one can live in the same household. These charts cover assistance recipients with enough information to calculate affordability, not all Australian renters.

Across Australia in March 2026, the share was 75.1% when calculated without assistance, and 44.0% after subtracting it from rent.

The difference is substantial. It also leaves a large group spending more than 30% of gross income on rent.

Putting the pieces together

A home is local.
The challenge is shared.

The Census shows how renting fits into Australian life: different buildings, different communities and different local costs. The assistance records reveal a narrower but important picture: support reduces the measured burden, yet many recipients remain under pressure.

These sources answer different questions. Together, they show why no single national rent, building type or assistance figure can describe everyone’s housing experience.

How to read the evidence

Census counts are adjusted to protect privacy, so components may not add exactly to totals. Composition layouts use the sum of their displayed cells. Exact national tenure shares use the published total.

Regional charts omit unavailable values, special non-geographic areas and Other Territories. Medians are not averages. Regional medians do not describe every resident and are not weighted by population.

CRA excludes cases without sufficient affordability data. “After assistance” uses (rent − assistance) ÷ gross income; “without” uses rent ÷ gross income. These definitions differ from measures restricted to low-income households.
